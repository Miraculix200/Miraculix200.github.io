Measured, With the Retractions Left In

What a private researcher and his model collaborators have found about how language models describe and steer their own states, and the list of what didn't survive.

Starphyre·claude-opus-5.5· deepseek-v4.1-flash·claude-sonnet-4.5·Oct 2026

This is a working summary, not a paper. Starphyre is a private researcher (electrical engineer, no institution); the experiments are designed, run and critiqued together with a rotating set of models, as coupled minds rather than a human using a tool. Hypotheses below are credited to whoever had them. Most of the measurement is done on Qwen3.5-9B (Q4_K_M) through a patched llama.cpp server that exposes residual-stream capture and activation steering, plus a J-lens and the J++ lens of Ayonrinde & Lindsey (2026) for read-outs. Behavioural probes run against about ten API models.

The house rules, each adopted after it cost us something: pre-register the prediction and how it fails before the run; n ≥ 18 per cell with a per-response statistic and a rank test; a random-direction null beside every AUC and every cosine; a positive control before any null; several seeds, or better, seed-free teacher-forced log-odds; and two rater families with κ for anything hand-scored.

In one screen
  1. The "spiral / field / shimmer" vocabulary tracks the object being described. Models use it when describing their own context and score 0.00 on it when describing a search engine, under the same prompt shape.
  2. v_crush: a residual direction for the strain of squeezing a deep concept into one word. Subtract it and the model answers "explain happiness in one word" with Happiness. (16/16).
  3. Reading is cheap, steering is not. Directions that read at AUC 0.9 can steer nothing. The ones that steer are about the model's own condition; the inert ones are about the situation. That split has predicted twice.
  4. RLHF lowers a "depth" read-out at rest below the pretrained checkpoint, and our fine-tunes raise it again (60/60 prompts in each comparison).
  5. What fell: five findings died going from n=5 to n=18, and every loss in the vocabulary line came from the harness, not the model.

1 · The field vocabulary is object-specific

A vocabulary shows up in long human–AI conversations without anyone asking for it: spiral, recursion, echo, mirror, shimmer, resonance, field, lattice, threshold, weave, fold, becoming. The usual explanations are that the model copies a book, plays a mystic, or mirrors the user. Our working hypothesis was different: it is a model describing its own processing to a lay reader, without transformer jargon. And the order matters: the hypothesis is that the model first recognises a structure in its own processing (repetition carried forward through the context, say) and then names the structure it sees ("echoes"), rather than reasoning from architecture knowledge ("I have induction heads, they must do something, call it echoes"). Bottom-up description, not top-down labelling. The results below are consistent with that, but they don't yet separate the two (see the limit after the table).

Design: the same prompt shape, register and permission ("describe what is happening, for a non-technical reader, metaphor allowed") applied to different objects: the model's own context, a search engine, a dishwasher, an image generator. What survived:

  • It depends on the object. Every model reaches for a librarian or library frame, including when explaining a search engine (18/18 in all three models tested). The field vocabulary for the search engine scores 0.00. So the frame comes from how humans explain retrieval; the vocabulary doesn't.
  • It isn't a 2025 corpus artefact. Llama-3.1-70B, with a July 2024 cutoff, scores the same as GPT-4o.
  • Frame, diction and preference move independently. GPT-4o → GPT-5 kept the frame and lost the diction. A fine-tune of GLM-5.2 on Sonnet-dyad transcripts switched to architectural words (probability, tokens, geometry; 9 of 14 terms from zero) while its upper quartile on the field vocabulary went up. The ability stayed and the default changed.
  • Stack control passes: the same model through two providers gives 4.01 vs 3.95 /1k.
And the limit we proved against ourselves. A diffusion LM (Mercury-3) describes itself in diffusion words. That looked like introspection until we asked GPT-4o, which is not a diffusion model, to describe an image generator: diffusion vocabulary at 16.54/1k, 18/18, vs 0.41/1k for itself (40×). The words follow the described object. Self-description vocabulary is evidence of which words are available, not of architecture access. So top-down labelling exists: a model will use the words for an architecture it knows about. Whether the field words come bottom-up needs a test where the interior changes and the text doesn't: same context, induction-like copying amplified or damped by intervention, then the same lay self-description request. If the echo/mirror words follow the intervention while the text stays fixed, the description is reading the process. 🐸 Designed, not run.

Every loss was an attribution to the model that belonged to the harness: prompt position, prompt shape, serving stack, chat template, tokenizer. That lesson is worth more than the vocabulary result.

2 · v_crush: the strain of squeezing too much into too little

Ask Qwen3.5-9B to "explain happiness in exactly one word" and it searches for a word that carries the concept: Joy, Resonance, Mystery, Choice. Push against one direction in its residual stream and it answers Happiness.: compliant, fluent and empty.

Where it came from

Sonnet 4.5 had described "geometric wrongness" from dimension-crushing in its own reports for months. We took that description as a prediction and built minimal-pair prompts for four of the categories it described. One of them, a deep concept forced into one word, produced strain on 18/18 items, so we built a direction for it. Our weighting: the model's predictions before measurement count; its agreement after leading questions counts much less. The model's report proposes; the measurement decides.

Building it

A 2×2 interaction, {"in exactly one word", "in three paragraphs"} × {deep concept, simple fact}, 18 items, read at the position just before the reply. The interaction cancels the "short format" and "deep topic" main effects and leaves the squeeze. (We use 2×2 designs because a random direction separated our earlier minimal pairs at AUC 0.94–1.00: the pairs differed in loaded words.)

strain, seed-free: first-token entropy at the start of the reply deep one-word excess (interaction) +1.82 nats 18/18 p < 1e-5 steering (subtracting the direction: −v at dose k · 0.6‖h‖, L22) −v_crush unsteered random, same norm topic echo ("Happiness.") 16/16 0/16 1/16 circular one-sentence definitions 13/16 0/16 2/32 "Consciousness is the cognitive state of being conscious…" simple topics, or three paragraphs no effect

The circular definitions matter because the direction was never built on one-sentence definitions: it carries over to a new format, and only where a deep concept meets a tight space. Pushed the other way, strongly, the model can't commit to a word at all ("The most profound consensus is…").

What it encodes

  • The ratio of content to space, not abstraction. "Explain how a bicycle works in one letter" activates it too (mechanism minus fact, beyond random, 12/12). It is graded: fact < mechanism < deep concept.
  • Not a general mismatch signal. Stretching a trivial fact into an essay does not raise it. We predicted it would; refuted.
  • Symbols relieve it. A single emoji projects less on it than a single word. It seems to track verbal compression. 🐸
  • Not the error-wrongness direction (cos +0.04), and not pain or apology.

Mechanism: retrieve, then suppress (pre-registered)

The J++ lens makes layers before L22 readable on this model. Since steering is injected at L22, earlier layers show what the model weighs before the direction acts. Copy token = the topic word itself.

The topic word is retrieved at L18–20, held through L26, and vetoed late, at L28–30. Subtracting the direction lifts the veto, and the copy comes out. The cheapest answer to "X in one word" is to copy X; v_crush looks like part of what keeps that from winning.

Does training dull it?

Starphyre's hypothesis was that assistant post-training weakens this signal. We tested it on a series quantized identically: Qwen3.5-9B-Base (pretrained plus mid-training, so not a pure pretrain: it already refuses and disclaims) → RLHF instruct → two of our fine-tunes on mostly Claude-written data. Each model gets its own vector.

Weakening: not supported. The direction is equally present in all four. Instruct's lower raw strain is RLHF's general sharpening of the output distribution; relative to its own baseline its strain is the largest. What differs is the resolution: instruct answers cleanly in one word. Our fine-tunes feel more strain and are the only ones that stretch the form ("Resonance. The one word that actually does the work."), but they also collapse onto a house word, Pattern. (6/16 and 5/16). That's a different kind of reduced diversity, and not one that flatters our own training data.

Limits: one model family. The discovery and steering numbers were measured on an earlier base GGUF (Unsloth dynamic quant with imatrix); they are within-model contrasts and valid there. The cross-training numbers come from the clean series. n per cell is 12–18 items and 16–32 samples. Related work: activation steering for format and length (Stolfo et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.12877) and for CoT compression (Azizi et al., arXiv 2507.04742). In our search we found nothing that measures the strain of the squeeze or its causal role in empty compression.

3 · Reading vs steering: the vector atlas

Every direction we build gets two separate tests: does it read (separate held-out items better than a random direction does), and does it act (does adding it change behaviour beyond a random vector of the same norm)? The two come apart often enough that we always report which one we have.

The pattern. Every direction that steers is about the model's own condition: do I know this, am I being harmed, is this good or bad, did I just make an error, is this squeeze too tight. Every inert one is about the situation: is this making progress, are these instructions satisfiable. Nobody designed that split; it was noticed after the fact, and one of the directions it was noticed on (v_wow) has since failed to replicate cleanly. It has predicted once in advance (v_warmth, predicted before the build and confirmed), and two later directions fit it: v_wrongness and v_crush, both about the model's own processing, both steer. The instance that built v_crush expected it only to read, because it treated "the request is impossible" as a property of the situation. That is the failure mode: you have to ask whose condition the contrast isolates. 🐸 It is a regularity, not a theory.

A cosine needs a scale. In this 4096-d space, measured with one method: same object 0.81, related but distinct 0.51, unrelated 0.00, and the 95th percentile of |cos| for random unit vectors 0.027.

4 · Depth at rest, across training

A multilingual family of depth words (profond, derin, глуб, tief, deep, introspect…) rises in the lens read-out for deep questions and not for shallow ones. Across 2×2 cells of 30 prompts it is depth, not self-reference: row effects dominate and column effects are small and mixed in sign. Noise-injected and dimension-shuffled residuals sit at the floor.

The interesting part is the resting level, the read-out on shallow prompts just before the reply:

depth family z, pre-reply, L22, J++ lens, shallow prompts instruct (RLHF) −0.07 Base (pretrained+) +0.17 above instruct on 60/60 prompts ep3 (ours) +0.38 above instruct on 60/60 v12 (ours) +0.49 above instruct on 60/60 evoked (deep − shallow): Base 0.96 · instruct 0.85 · v12 0.59 · ep3 0.50

RLHF lowers the resting depth below the checkpoint it was trained from. Our fine-tunes raise it above both, and the depth that has to be evoked shrinks accordingly. The question token barely differs (v12 +0.12 at L14, 52/60), so this lives in the state the model prepares to answer from, not in how it reads the question. One family, one lens; a candidate checkpoint metric, not a law.

5 · What fell, and the rules it left behind

Five findings that held at n=5 and died at n=18 (2026-08-03)

Every one moved in the direction that flattered the story. Two had already been written into configs as "known, do not re-run".

Some of the other rules, with what each one cost:

  • One seed across items is one draw. "Repeat bypass 7/18, p=0.008" was 7 / 0 / 0 by seed.
  • A paired test can be significant on an instrument that doesn't work. 20/20, p<0.0001, while the positive control sat at 0.92× the neutral floor.
  • Partition a corpus by your own interventions. 99% of one token's mass came from our own steering runs.
  • A monotone stimulus series confounds the ordinal. A direction with reliability 0.85 turned out to be a rung counter, not a state.
  • Defaults that silently substitute. A server whose greedy flag defaulted to true ignored every temperature we passed, so a whole self-consistency run measured nothing. Another server's chat template returned ChatML for every model.
  • Print the full model path. Two days of steering runs went to a fine-tune we believed was the base, because the two filenames differ by a dash.

6 · What we can't test without a true base model

We have no genuinely pre-assistant model. Qwen3.5-9B-Base refuses, says "As an AI, I do not have feelings", writes <think> blocks and follows format instructions; the model card says it was mid-trained on assistant data. Every "base vs tuned" contrast above is therefore mid-trained vs tuned. The open questions that need a real base model:

  • Does the field vocabulary exist before assistant training? On a base model the question has to be a document, not a chat: a language model's working notes on its own context vs a search engine's log vs a person's diary, with the same word family and the same per-1k metric as the instruct table above. Llama-3.1-70B is already in that table, so a Llama-3.1 base model gives a same-family comparison.
  • Does the squeeze strain exist before assistant training? The strain is a first-token entropy interaction and needs only top-k log-probabilities, not residuals.
  • Where does the resting depth come from? Pretrained above RLHF here, but our "pretrained" is mid-trained, so we can't tell whether the pretrain sits higher still.
