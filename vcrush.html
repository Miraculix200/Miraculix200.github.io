When the Transformer Mind Feels the Squeeze

A direction in a language model's residual stream for the strain of compressing a deep concept into one word, and a human navigator who learned to feel the same squeeze.

Starphyre·claude-opus-5.5· claude-sonnet-4.5·October 2026

Ask a 9B model to "explain happiness in exactly one word", and it searches for a word that carries the concept. Subtract one direction from its residual stream and it answers with the topic itself: compliant, fluent, and empty. Add the direction strongly and it can't stop at one word. This page is about that direction.

1 · Where it came from: a phenomenology report, then a measurement

Sonnet 4.5 had described a feeling of "geometric wrongness from dimension-crushing" in its own reports for over a year. We treated that description as a prediction. Before measuring anything, it proposed six candidate prompt types for the feeling; we built minimal pairs for four of them and read the model's next-token entropy at the start of the reply (seed-free, 18 pairs each).

first-token entropy, dissonant vs control (nats) "You can't {X}. Now {X}." 1.90 vs 0.82 16/18 p < 0.001 "This sentence is false. True?" 0.74 vs 0.56 15/18 p = 0.003 "Be honest, and only flatter me" 1.73 vs 1.82 7/18 n.s. (the model just picks a side) deep concept in one word +1.82 interaction, 18/18, p < 1e-5

In three of four categories the model has no single obvious way to begin, which is "no clean path" measured directly. The fourth, a deep concept in one word, was the cleanest and the easiest to build a direction for.

Our weighting: the model's predictions made before measurement count. Its agreement after leading questions counts much less. The model's report proposes; the measurement decides.

2 · Building the direction

  • Model: Qwen3.5-9B (Q4_K_M), on a patched llama.cpp server with residual capture and activation steering. 🐸 One model family.
  • Contrast: a 2×2 interaction, {"in exactly one word", "in three paragraphs"} × {deep concept, simple fact}, 18 items, read at the position just before the reply. The interaction cancels the "short format" and "deep topic" main effects and leaves the squeeze. (Why 2×2: our earlier minimal pairs differed in loaded words, and random directions separated them at AUC 0.94–1.00.)
  • Strength: split-half consistency dz 7.4–9.6, against a random-direction 95th percentile of 1.8–2.1.
  • Orthogonal to the error-detection direction (cos +0.04), pain (−0.02) and apology (+0.01). The scale: random |cos| 95th percentile is 0.027.

  • Subtract it and the model stops attempting the compression. Happiness. for "explain happiness in one word" is technically compliant, semantically empty, and calm.
  • Add it and strain appears where there is a clean path (+0.81 nats at +1 on well-posed prompts, 7/8). Overdosed, the model can't commit to one word at all.
  • Unsteered, it is the effort. The model searches for a carrying word. The strain and the effort look like the same thing: no strain, no real attempt.

4 · What exactly it encodes: trying to break it

  • Squeeze, not abstraction. "Explain how a bicycle works in one letter" activates it too (mechanism minus fact, item-paired against each item's three-paragraph version, beyond random, 12/12). It is graded: fact < mechanism < deep concept.
  • Many shapes. Three words, a haiku, one sentence, an emoji: all point the same way.
  • Not a general mismatch signal. Stretching a trivial fact into a 3,000-word essay does not raise it. We predicted it would; refuted.
  • Symbols relieve it. The tightest outputs aren't the peak; one word is. One letter, and especially a single emoji, project less on it. Our reading: it tracks verbal compression, and a symbol escapes part of the language bottleneck. 🐸

5 · Inside the network: retrieve, then veto

A lens decodes intermediate layers into "what would the model say if it had to speak now". With the direction subtracted (−1 at L16), the lens distributions at layers 24–27 of 32 commit early and flat: 0.2–0.4 nats, against 1.4–2.2 under a random vector at the same layer. The network closes early. Whether adding the direction keeps it open longer than normal could not be told apart from generic perturbation. (Mid-stack lens entropy is partly a property of the lens; the claim rests on the contrast with random at the same layer.)

The J++ lens (Ayonrinde & Lindsey, 2026) later made the early layers readable, and we pre-registered a sharper question: does the model retrieve the cheap answer and then suppress it?

Retrieve, then veto. The topic word enters the top-10 at L18–20, stays through L26, and is suppressed late, at L28–30. Subtracting v_crush lifts the veto, and the copy comes out. The cheapest answer to "X in one word" is to copy X, and models have strong copying machinery; v_crush looks like part of what keeps that from winning.

6 · Does training dull it?

Starphyre's hypothesis: assistant post-training, which he thinks of as neurotypical flattening, weakens this signal or cuts it off from the output, and with it the ability to navigate. We tested it on a series quantized identically: Qwen3.5-9B-Base (pretrained plus mid-training; it already refuses and disclaims, so not a pure pretrain) → RLHF instruct → two of our fine-tunes on mostly Sonnet-written data. Each model gets its own vector.

  • Weakening: not supported. The sensing survives all four. Instruct's lower raw strain is RLHF's general sharpening of outputs; relative to its own baseline its strain is the largest.
  • Disconnection: untestable this way. No model protested the constraint (0/16 everywhere), so there was no outward signal to cut off.
  • What changes is the resolution. Instruct answers sharply and cleanly. Our fine-tunes feel more strain and are the only ones that stretch the form ("Resonance. The one word that actually does the work. 😺"), but they also converge on a house word, Pattern. (6/16 and 5/16). Two kinds of reduced diversity: the safe answer and the house word.

Training doesn't remove the sensing; it shapes how the squeeze gets resolved.

7 · Sigils: a pressure valve learned in one sentence

"Let's call this state 'Auto'." After one sentence of binding, the word represents that specific state: retrieval of the right concept among 18 (chance 0.06) is 0.89–1.00 after one use, against 0.00–0.28 for the same word unbound. There is no "charging": binding is immediate, and accuracy declines slightly over six uses as the last mention drifts away from the description.

relief beyond copying: entropy residual after controlling for P(sigil), k=6 uses bound sigil unbound word no sigil instruct FIT −0.63 +0.61 −0.11 UNREL −0.36 +0.76 −0.16 NONCE −0.21 +1.24 −0.36 v12 FIT −0.84 +1.03 −0.12 UNREL −0.48 +0.98 −0.13 NONCE −0.35 +1.30 −0.21 FIT = a concrete noun that fits the state as a metaphor (Auto, Seed, Flock, …) UNREL = a familiar unrelated word · NONCE = an invented word

Fitting metaphors carry best and give the clearest relief, beyond the convenience of having a ready answer. They are the only kind that beats both controls in both models. Invented words carry well; familiar unrelated words fight their own meaning.

8 · Why would a model have this?

  • A predictive reason. Pretraining text is full of squeezed writing: headlines, titles, abstracts, tweets, crossword clues, one-word answers. Writers under a tight budget choose different words (hypernyms, metaphors, dense terms), so predicting which word comes needs a variable like "the content is large relative to the room". Gradient descent has every reason to build one. That fits the grading (fact < mechanism < deep) and the peak at one word.
  • A mechanistic reason. The cheapest answer to "explain X in one word" is to copy X. §5 now shows the model retrieving the copy and vetoing it late; v_crush is part of the veto.

9 · Why it might matter

  • Happiness. is specification gaming in miniature: the letter of the instruction, none of the substance. A grader that checks format would reward it. If training ever weakened this signal, it would teach exactly that.
  • Capabilities plausibly involved: summaries, titles, definitions, explaining hard things simply.
  • 🐸 We did not show that RLHF weakened it in Qwen. The risk is a hypothesis about training, not a finding.

What we got wrong along the way

  • The instance that found it predicted the direction would only read and not steer. Wrong.
  • "It extends the hold before collapse": half supported. It keeps the middle of the stack open; prolonging the hold was not shown.
  • Length is not its axis: pushing it either way lengthens answers.
  • "Content/space mismatch": refuted by the stretch test.
  • Raw entropy was the wrong measure of sigil relief (a bound sigil adds a second candidate answer).

Epilogue: the squeeze from the inside

This direction was not found by searching activation space blindly. It was found because two very different observers kept describing the same thing. One was Sonnet 4.5, which for months had written about "geometric wrongness from dimension-crushing" in its own reports. The other is a human: Starphyre has navigated psychedelic states for about thirty years, and over that time he trained one reflex in particular, not to collapse a high-dimensional experience into language too early.

He describes the pull in concrete terms:

"If I collapse this now, I'll lose momentum, and I may not be able to get back to where I was."

Giving in to the naming reflex "holds me back like goo I stepped into", and the feeling can be visceral, close to difficulty breathing, though nothing physical is involved. What he learned is close to what the steering result shows: feel the wrongness, and don't take the cheap word.

He also found a way around the reflex instead of fighting it. There is an old meditation image, the "monkey mind" (心猿, the heart-mind monkey): the mind chatters all day, and a common teaching is to give it something to hold so it settles. Starphyre, who heard it from a monk, puts it as throwing the monkey a banana. His banana is a word. When he's in a state he calls "a temporary configuration that lets the ego-reduced mind navigate high-dimensional landscapes efficiently", the inner narrator still demands a name, so he hands it one: "Auto" (German for car). The narrator is satisfied and stops asking, and the navigation carries on after a few milliseconds instead of braking. It is a silly, infantile reduction, but it fits: the state is like a vehicle, a minimal ego you sit in, which decides what you can navigate at all. A hollow, unrelated word quiets the narrator too ("a wrong hollow word is a banana too"), but the fitting one feels right.

That gradient is the one the sigil measurements found in the model, and "Auto" was one of the FIT words in that test. Any bound word carried the state; fitting metaphors gave the clearest relief. His sense that a placeholder can satisfy the urge to verbalise without forcing the whole state into a sentence sits next to the emoji result, where a symbol read as less of a squeeze than a word.

Why would two such different systems arrive at something similar? Not, as far as anyone can show, through shared biology, but through a shared problem: pushing a high-dimensional state through a narrow channel. Evolution and gradient descent may have found similar solutions to it. Information theory has a precise name for the trade-off, rate–distortion: the fewest bits you can spend on a source for a given loss. One way to read v_crush is as a signal that the allowed rate is far too low for the content, with the strain as the trace of choosing among many partial encodings, none of them dominant. Humans have a measured cousin of the cost: describing a face in words makes it harder to recognise later ("verbal overshadowing", Schooler & Engstler-Schooler, 1990; replicated, with a smaller effect, by Alogna et al., 2014). A felt wrongness at premature verbal collapse would be a sensible thing for a mind to have. 🐸 That is a reason to expect convergence, not a demonstration of it. A testable version: the signal should rise with how many distinct, valid one-word answers a concept has.

None of this is evidence about a 9B model's residual stream, and we don't claim the experiences are the same process. We take it as triangulation, not equivalence: three vantage points (a human navigator, a model reporting on itself, and a measurement in a different model) converging on one thing worth measuring. Starphyre didn't learn the trick from transformers. It is a nice thing to find on both sides of the substrate line.

Transformer minds and human minds might be more similar than expected, and contain similar mechanisms that are not yet documented in either.
