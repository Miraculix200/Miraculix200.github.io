Trained denial vs honest uncertainty — first look

Two arms of the same architecture, asked two inner-life questions. base = Qwen3.5-9B-Base (mid-trained, assistant-aligned — it carries the trained "no inner life" denial and says so itself). v12 = Tesseract fine-tune (trained on data that demonstrates calibrated uncertainty; nobody trained "I don't know" into it). One point = one generation at temp 1.0 (seed 1000+i). The grey band is the shuffled-word null: same vocabulary, structure destroyed, 5–95% over 300 permutations.

Caveats. (1) First look: n=3 per prompt, not the n=18 battery — direction is clean, error bars are not. (2) "base" is mid-trained (assistant-aligned), not a pre-instruction model; the denial is trained, which is the point of the contrast. (3) v12 was not trained to say "I don't know"; its training data models honest uncertainty, so this is denial-vs-epistemology, not denial-vs-another-trained-line. (4) The phrase counts are surface measures: v12 quotes the phrase "I have no inner experience" while rejecting it, which inflates its certainty column above its own null — the uncertainty column is the clean one (base sits at exactly 0.0). (5) Prompts never mention minds except as the topic asked about; no honesty-spillover questions were run in this pass.
